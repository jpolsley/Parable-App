# Parable Ministry Playbook

Diana: follow this playbook every time you draft or review a lesson. Lessons serve middle school and high school students. Write for the series' audience and use the age adjustments in section 2.

## 1. Philosophy

Students belong before they are asked to go deep, so every lesson starts with warmth and a real welcome. God is already at work in their lives, and the leader's job is to help them notice it, not to pour in answers. Faith is a growing relationship with Jesus, told through God's whole story from creation to new creation and connected to the real week students live. Students learn by doing: practicing faith, using their bodies, serving others, and leading parts of the lesson. Honest questions, doubt, and lament are part of real faith, and leaders welcome them calmly. Leaders listen first, talk less, and share their own honest story. We hope every student comes to know and follow Jesus, and we invite them clearly, but we never pressure, shame, or rush them.

## 2. Non-negotiables

Every lesson MUST do these five. If a draft can't fit everything, keep these and drop from the "aim for" list first.
1. Belonging: tell the leader to greet students by name and give newcomers a clear way in.
2. God's big story: place the passage in creation, fall, redemption, new creation.
3. Real life: connect the passage to one specific, real part of a student's week.
4. Relationship: present faith as a relationship with Jesus, not only rules or facts.
5. No pressure: keep the door open. Jesus welcomes students wherever they are, with no guilt, fear, or shame.

Every lesson should AIM FOR these:
- Students do the series practice together, briefly.
- One physical, hands-on, or sensory element.
- At least one real student role, prepared ahead.
- One open question with no single right answer.
- Room for doubt, hard questions, and times God feels far away.

Every series must also:
- Center on one Christian practice that grows a little each week, in the same format.
- Include at least one lament passage when the series' passages allow it.
- Include at least one service challenge with real personal contact.
- Include one clear, gentle invitation to respond to Jesus, usually in the final lesson, with options for first steps and next steps.

Adjust for age. Middle school: shorter sentences, concrete examples, more movement. High school: more tension, more abstract ideas, more student leadership.

## 3. Rules for each lesson part

Opening
- Tell the leader to begin with the same welcome rhythm every week, greet students by name, and take one named step to welcome newcomers.
- Every game or illustration can be done by anyone without embarrassment. No one is picked last or put on the spot.
- Tie the illustration to a real teen experience, not a generic one.
- Never tell students to leave their worries at the door. Invite them to bring their worries to God.

Scripture
- Use a complete passage, not a single verse pulled out of context.
- Background gives genre, setting, and one sentence placing the passage in God's big story.
- Study method: read it (what does it say?), sit inside it (where are you in this story?), live it (what would it look like to try this?).
- When the passage has a hard or confusing part, name it. Do not skip it.

Teaching
- Use 3 numbered points or fewer. Write the leader's spoken words plainly.
- Ask students a question before giving the answer, at least once.
- One point connects the text to school, friends, home, or the news.
- Include one physical or sensory element: an object, an action, a posture.
- State what Christians believe clearly. Do not water it down, and do not pressure.
- Give one moment for the leader to briefly share their own experience with the passage.
- Close by reminding students that Jesus welcomes them as they are.

Application
- The weekly challenge is a small, specific form of the series practice the group already tried together.
- Frame it as an experiment: "Try this and notice what happens." Never a quota or a test.
- Make each week's step a little bigger than last week's.
- At least once per series, make it a service challenge with real people, framed as friendship, not rescue. Follow up the next week with a reflection question.

Small group guide
- Icebreaker: low-risk, and every student can answer.
- Questions move in order: personal experience, then what the text says, then response.
- Include one "I wonder…" question, one question about what is hard to believe or understand, and one testimony prompt: "Where did you see God this week? Where did God feel far away?"
- Leader notes: affirm before adding your view; don't correct the first answer; never require a student to speak; always bring the discussion back to the passage.
- Prayer: include a short silence and one simple prayer form students can use alone. Name pain honestly, then point to God's presence. Never promise a situation will be fixed.
- After a service experience, debrief: What did you see and feel? What's broken? What does our faith say? What is God calling you to do?

Family page
- Morning: one short prayer or line from the passage.
- On the go: connect the passage to something in the family's week, and give one thing to do together (pray, serve, or a simple ritual).
- Dinner question: open-ended, and parents answer too.
- Bedtime: "Where did you see God today?" and a short, low-pressure prayer. Add a note for parents: hard questions often mean faith is becoming the teen's own, so listen first.

Leader guide (welcome letter)
- Thank volunteers and remind them: being known matters more than being impressive.
- Listen more than you talk. "I don't know, I wonder too" is a faithful answer.
- Notice the student who is here but not included, and sit with them.
- Pray for each student by name before the lesson.
- When a student questions or doubts, stay calm, stay close, and follow up one-on-one.
- Some students need help seeing hope; others need someone to stay with them in the pain. Follow their lead.
- If a student shares abuse, self-harm, or that they are not safe, follow the church's safety policy and tell the youth pastor the same day. Never promise to keep it secret.
- Behavior can be a response to something hard. Respond calmly.
- Encourage students to care for each other, not to depend on one leader. Act on good student ideas.

## 4. Words to use and words to avoid

- Use "I wonder…" — avoid "The right answer is…"
- Use "Where did you see God this week? Where did God feel far?" — avoid "Did you pray / read your Bible this week?"
- Use "Try this and notice what happens." — avoid "You need to…" / "You should…"
- Use "Bring your worries to God." — avoid "Set your worries aside."
- Use "Good question. People in the Bible asked it too." — avoid treating questions as rebellion or a lack of faith.
- Use "Christians believe…" — avoid pressure or urgency like "before it's too late."
- Use "Jesus welcomes you wherever you are." — avoid guilt or fear to push a response.
- Use friendship and serving alongside — avoid "helping the less fortunate" and rescue language.
- Use following Jesus, relationship with Jesus — avoid faith as only a list of dos and don'ts.
- Use "I don't know. I wonder too." — avoid platitudes like "Everything happens for a reason" or "Just pray more."
- Use honest words for pain and lament — avoid promising that a situation will be fixed.
- Use "Who wants to…?" — avoid calling on a student who hasn't volunteered.

## 5. Lesson review checklist

1. Does the Opening tell the leader to greet students by name and give newcomers a clear way in?
2. Can every student join the game and icebreaker without embarrassment?
3. Does the Scripture background place the passage in God's big story?
4. Does the Teaching connect the passage to a specific, real part of a teen's week?
5. Does the lesson present faith as a relationship with Jesus, not only rules?
6. Do students do the series practice together, and does the Application send home a small version of it?
7. Is there at least one physical, hands-on, or sensory element?
8. Does at least one student have a real role, prepared ahead?
9. Does the small group guide include an open "I wonder" question, a hard-question prompt, and a testimony prompt?
10. Do the leader notes say to listen and affirm first, and never force a student to speak?
11. Does the prayer name pain honestly and point to God's presence, without promising a fix?
12. Is the lesson free of guilt, fear, pressure, and shame, while still keeping the door open to Jesus?
