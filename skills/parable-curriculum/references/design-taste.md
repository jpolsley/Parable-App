# Justin's design taste

These rules come from his reactions to real sample pages. They're his feedback, not general design advice. When a
rule and a reference picture disagree, ask.

## The big rules

1. **Take the aesthetic, not the layout.** When Justin sends a reference image, he wants its *feel* translated into
   an original composition.
   - What happened: a cover that copied the Proof Sheet poster's stacked boxes and hatched panels got "I didn't want
     the actual look. I wanted it in the aesthetic."
   - So describe the feel in a few words first: palette, type personality, density, the one loud element, texture,
     motifs. Then design something new from those qualities.
   - Never reuse the reference's artwork, logos or characters, for example Apple's stickers. Draw originals in that
     style.
2. **Type leads; graphics support.** In the Sticker Shop round he said the stickers were "TOO central."
   - What he liked in the FUNDAMENTAL poster:
     - giant type as the main event
     - stickers and stars as accents on the edges, cropped by the page and tucked behind or over the title
     - layered depth
   - Keep icons small and at the edges.
3. **Loud outside, calm inside.**
   - **What happened:** Justin asked for a pass carrying the cover's motifs onto every inside page: tinted pages,
     tilted chips, wavy dividers, starburst bullets and halftone strips. His reaction: "Actually I hate that."
   - **The approach he agreed to:** covers and week dividers carry all the energy, and inside pages stay white and
     readable. Leaders glance at them mid-sentence.
   - **Inside pages may borrow a few things:**
     - the display font for section titles, in capitals on a highlight
     - small flat label tags (SAY, DO, ASK) instead of tiny gray text
     - stage-cue tags ("HOLD UP THE CHARGER")
     - the black big-idea box
4. **No stock "AI-looking" icons.** Thin line icons in pastel circles ("symbols in use", "inside each week") felt
   fake.
   - Use words, big stamped numbers (01, 02, 03) or label tags instead.
   - Drop a symbols legend if the pages already label things in words.
5. **Each week's title page is different.** Don't give five dividers one template with swapped text.
   - Vary the background color, where the title sits, where the giant number goes (behind, sideways, tilted) and where
     the badge sits.
   - In a layout file, style each week from its number (see `layout-files.md`).
6. **Make it look printed.** He liked:
   - paper grain
   - fold creases
   - worn corners
   - halftone dots that really change size (real halftone, not a fade)
   - hard offset shadows
   - screen-printed outlines on cut-paper shapes
7. **Show, then build.** Make sample pages (a PDF of the real book printed with the design) and let him react. Don't
   add a design to the app's built-in library until he picks it.

## Interior page preferences (the default Parable book)

From the early print rounds, these are already built into Parable's default print design. Don't undo them.

- **Organized by who uses each page:**
  - cover
  - "Start here" leader guide
  - for each week:
    - a full-page divider
    - session plan
    - session overview table
    - large group lesson
    - **one-page small group guide**
    - **family page** with four everyday-moment cards
- **Each main section starts at the top of a new page.**
- **Layout and spacing:**
  - No awkward gaps: text flows to fill pages, headings stay with their first lines, and slightly long sections shrink
    to fit instead of leaving a few lines stranded on a page.
  - No "Bottom line" banner at the top of the lesson. The big idea already appears on the session plan and divider.
  - Section headers: a small line ("Part 3 · 16 min"), then the title large, then a rule. A condensed all-caps
    header with a big section number clashing with the "01 02 03" point numbers "felt off."
- **Text styling:**
  - One small margin label per block (SAY, DO, ASK, READ, NEED, NOTE).
  - Spoken words in serif, instructions in smaller sans.
- **Small group page:** full width, with "Pray together" and "This week" side by side, all on one page.

## What he chose

- **Poster Club** (round 3) is his favorite and is built into Parable. When he later sent its PDF, he said "No this
  was the best."
  - **Cover:** hot pink and red poster, with a giant dark ghost "B.L.E.S.S." behind a crisp cream title.
  - **Ribbons and stars:** swooping red ribbons, and spiky green and blue stars cropped at the edges.
  - **Stacked echo labels** with a hard shadow.
  - **Finish:** grain, folds and worn corners.
  - **Dividers:** each week different:
    - week 1: pink with a red band
    - week 2: blue and flipped
    - week 3: green with a sideways number
    - week 4: red with a tilted number
    - week 5: a cream finale

    Each has a small halftone sticker badge (praying hands, headphones, taco, heart in hand, speech bubble with a
    cross).
  - **Inside:** white pages, section titles in caps on a pink highlight with small starbursts, and green number boxes.
  - **Palette:** pink `#F43FCB`, red `#EE3427`, green `#33D35B`, blue `#2F66FF`, yellow `#F8E63C`, cream `#F6EFE3`,
    ink `#16111A`.
  - **Display face:** Plus Jakarta Sans ExtraBold, tight tracking.
  - **Limit:** the stickers are tied to B.L.E.S.S.'s week numbers. A new series needs its own sticker set.
- **Spine** is built in too, made from a FREITAG reference: a near-black board, a blue spine stripe, a huge vertical
  title, outlined label boxes and condensed caps. Justin used it as the first layout file; he hasn't said it's a
  favorite.
- **Not chosen:**
  - Sticker Shop: stickers too central.
  - The "loud interior" Poster Club pass: hated.
  - The "calm interior" Poster Club variant: never adopted, since he went back to round 3.
  - The Proof Sheet cover: copied the layout.
  - The Spec Sheet cover: "I don't love it."
  - The Liquid Frame cover: shown last, no verdict yet.

  The files are in `assets/layouts/` in case a piece is ever wanted again.

## Aesthetics he has shown interest in

- Apple-style sticker sheets: neon cut-paper stickers with black halftone, on a black board or light gray.
- Riso and screen-printed posters: FUNDAMENTAL (hot pink, red, green, blue), giant type, stars.
- Technical spec-sheet posters: crop marks, + marks, monospace metadata, one loud element, hatching, slight
  off-register color.
- FORMLESS-style posters: a strict symmetric frame of small bold caps and rules around one wild, hand-made piece of
  type.
- FREITAG-style: dark board, a vertical title like a book spine, outlined label boxes.

## Printing realities to mention

- Hot pink and neon print duller and darker on office printers. Keep heavy ink on covers and dividers only.
- Background graphics must be on in the print dialog. Chrome prints Parable best.
